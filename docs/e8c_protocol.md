# E8c protocol

In the Mnemonic Similarity Task, young adults call 40.3% of similar new pictures old (Stark and colleagues 2013, Table 2). E8b gave the fully local hippocampus, at human cell proportions, the same test built from Omniglot drawings, with two readouts, and neither matched people. The CA1 comparator (R1) reached REC 0.724 but called almost no lure old (p(old | lure) 0.024 on the closer lures) and its lure discrimination index (LDI) was 0.006, against people's 0.360. The readout that judged from CA3's settled state (R2) called 39.6% of closer lures old, close to people, but for the wrong reason: attractor dynamics carried every cue, foils included, onto some stored pattern, so R2's two thresholds crossed and it never answered "similar". One logged quantity did separate the item types: the support that CA3's starting state, before any attractor step, gets from its own recurrent synapses (median 0.233 for repeats, 0.049 to 0.052 for lures and foils). It was logged only and never used to decide.

E8c asks whether a familiarity signal read from CA3's starting state, before attractor dynamics run, makes human-shaped recognition errors. It changes one thing, the readout, and no part of the circuit. Because the idea came from looking at E8b's evaluation results, E8c is scored on lists built from evaluation drawings that E8b's main lists never used, with seeds E8b never used. This file states the biological case, the readout, the test set and its limits, every setting, the runs and the predictions. It was committed before any run. The sources, with quotes and locations, are in `docs/e8c_sources.md`; the human rates and lesion findings are in `docs/e8_sources.md`; E8b's model is in `docs/e8b_protocol.md`.

## Terms

- **Mnemonic Similarity Task (MST)**. A person studies pictures once, then sees **repeats** (the same picture), **lures** (a similar picture never shown) and **foils** (new pictures), and answers old, similar or new for each (Stark and colleagues 2013). **LDI** = p(similar | lure) − p(similar | foil). **REC** = p(old | repeat) − p(old | foil). Young adults (20 to 39 years): LDI 0.360, REC 0.786, p(old | lure) 0.403. Adults 60 to 74: p(old | lure) 0.546 (same table).
- **The human shape of lure errors.** The more similar a lure is to the studied picture, the more often people call it old and the less often they tell it apart: lures in the most similar bin "elicit false alarms quite often" and those in the least similar bin "have very few false alarms" (Stark and colleagues 2019), so LDI rises from the most similar lures to the least similar.
- **Pattern separation** stores similar experiences as distinct patterns; in the model DG does this, giving each drawing its own sparse CA3 pattern. **Attractor dynamics** in CA3: over repeated rounds, cells supported by stored recurrent synapses stay active and the rest drop out, until activity holds still at an **attractor**, a stored pattern or a mixture of them. Rebuilding a whole stored pattern from part of it this way is **pattern completion**.
- **Starting state.** When a test drawing arrives, its entorhinal (EC) code drives CA3 through the perforant path, and the 2% most driven CA3 cells fire (k-winners-take-all, the model's stand-in for feedback inhibition). This is CA3's state before any recurrent step; attractor dynamics start from it.
- **Familiarity and recollection.** Dual-process accounts split recognition into familiarity, a graded sense that an item was met before, and recollection, retrieval of the earlier episode (Yonelinas 2002). In network models, familiarity is often read from the **energy** of the presented pattern in a recurrent network, E = −½ Σ w_ij x_i x_j, before any retrieval step; stored patterns have lower energy than new ones (Bogacz, Brown and Giraud-Carrier 2001).
- **The model** is E8b's, unchanged: DG, CA3 and CA1 at human proportions scaled by 1/500 (30,000, 3,400 and 32,000 cells), 2% active, mossy fibres from DG, CA3 with clipped binary recurrent synapses, a one-shot perforant path, attractor dynamics by k-winners-take-all until the state stops changing, and CA1 learning by behavioural timescale synaptic plasticity (`docs/e8b_protocol.md`).
- **R1** (E8b): the cosine between the test drawing's EC code and the EC code CA1 returns. **R2** (E8b): the self-support of CA3's settled state, the share of the active cells' potentiated recurrent input that comes from other active cells.

## The biological case

The sources are read in full in `docs/e8c_sources.md`, with pages or sections. In short:

- **The computation is supported.** Several models read familiarity from a recurrent network's energy for the presented pattern, with no retrieval step. Bogacz, Brown and Giraud-Carrier (2001, p. 16): "the neurons do not perform any computations (there is no relaxation), but the familiarity discrimination is done by checking the value of the energy function after delivering a pattern". Greve and colleagues (2009, section 1.1): "there is no dynamics in the network". Cortes and colleagues (2010) read the energy at the moment the probe is loaded and show that it stops telling old from new "after a short transient of some five time units", because "the activity in the Hopfield network will always reach an attractor state regardless of the initial activity pattern" (section "Dynamics of the Familiarity Discriminators"). That is what E8b saw: attractor dynamics carried every cue onto a stored pattern, and only the starting state kept the item types apart. Hopfield (1982, p. 2558) already noted that familiar and unfamiliar starting states differ in their initial processing, a property readable only "by a class of neurons or devices abstracting average properties of the processing group". Familiarity read this way also holds far more items than recall: 0.023N² patterns for N neurons against about 0.145N for retrieval (Bogacz and colleagues 2001, p. 11).
- **What a downstream population could receive.** Cortes and colleagues write the energy as E = −Σ_i h_i s_i, the input each active cell receives times its activity, and propose that "an additional set of neurons" compute each product and that "their activities are then summed in an output neuron to yield the energy". For CA3 this is the summed recurrent drive that the starting state's active cells receive from one another in the first recurrent pass. In Hasselmo, Wyble and Wallenstein's model (1996), "familiar input patterns caused higher levels of activity in region CA3 due to a match with previously strengthened connections". Which population reads CA3's first-pass drive is not identified in any source read here.
- **The location is not supported.** Every familiarity model read places the energy readout in perirhinal cortex, not the hippocampus (Bogacz and colleagues 2001; Bogacz and Brown 2003; Cortes and colleagues 2010), on single-unit, gene-expression and lesion evidence: response reductions to repeated items in about 25% of perirhinal neurons but under about 1% of hippocampal neurons, with longer latencies there (Brown and Aggleton 2001, p. 52). Accounts of novelty inside the hippocampus place the comparison in CA1, between what CA3 recalls and what EC sends (Hasselmo and Schnell 1994; Hasselmo and colleagues 1996; Lisman and Grace 2005; Duncan and colleagues 2012), and Kumaran and Maguire (2007) found that hippocampal mismatch signals are "contingent on the process of hippocampal recall" and "argue strongly against the operation of a familiarity mechanism within the hippocampus". In Norman and O'Reilly's model (2003), the hippocampal signal is computed after recall, as match minus mismatch, and pattern separation keeps lures near floor until they are very similar to a studied item.
- **Timing fits a first-pass readout.** Theta-rhythm models put encoding in the phase when EC input is strong and CA3's recurrent input weak, and retrieval in the phase when recurrent input is strong (Hasselmo, Bodelón and Wyble 2002, p. 793); acetylcholine, which rises for novel input in Hasselmo and Schnell's model (1994, p. 3911), suppresses CA3's recurrent synapses more than its perforant-path synapses at low concentrations (Kremin and Hasselmo 2007). The starting state is CA3 under EC drive; its first recurrent pass is the first moment the stored synapses act on it.
- **What a CA3-located signal predicts for lures.** In human high-resolution fMRI, CA3 and DG respond to lures almost as to new pictures, while CA1, subiculum and the cortex around them respond to lures much as to repeats (Bakker and colleagues 2008: bias 0.15 in CA3/DG against 0.59 to 0.83 elsewhere). A familiarity signal read in CA3, downstream of DG's pattern separation, should therefore treat most lures like foils.

**Verdict.** No source read places a familiarity readout in CA3's first recurrent pass. The literature supports the computation (the energy of the starting state, read before attractor dynamics by a population that sums each active cell's recurrent input) and places it in perirhinal cortex; it places the hippocampus's own novelty signal in CA1, after recall. The model has no perirhinal cortex, and CA3 is its only recurrent network. R3 is therefore labelled a **stand-in**: the familiarity computation the literature supports, applied to CA3's recurrent synapses, with the reading population unidentified. The hippocampal readout the literature supports most directly is the CA1 comparator, R1, which E8c keeps and reports. Reading the same energy from a perirhinal stand-in built on the EC code is the readout the location evidence points to; it is outside E8c and is listed under "Not in E8c".

## The change: a starting-state readout (R3)

1. **R3.** CA3 receives the perforant cue as E8b computes it (the starting state). With A its active cells, n = |A| and W[i, j] the recurrent synapse from cell i onto cell j,

   R3 = (sum over j in A, i in A of W[i, j]) / (n (n − 1)),

   the mean recurrent input an active cell receives from the other active cells, as a share of the most it could receive. With clipped binary synapses it is the share of the starting state's own cell pairs whose synapse is potentiated. It equals the state's energy with the sign reversed, divided by a constant (n is 68 for every item, the k-winners-take-all count). Old when R3 is high, similar when middling, new when low. It is computed before any attractor step, from the synapses and the cells now firing, and never compares the state with a list of stored memories.
2. **R3s**, scored and reported beside it: E8b's self-support formula (R2's) applied to the starting state instead of the settled state. This is the quantity E8b logged as "cue self-support", the one that suggested E8c. It divides the within-state input by all potentiated input onto the active cells, so cells that belong to many stored patterns count against it. R3 is the form the familiarity models use; R3s is kept so the signal that prompted E8c is tested on clean data too. The predictions are written for R3.
3. **R1 and R2** are computed exactly as in E8b, from the same lists and wiring, and reported in full.
4. **Lesions.** Without CA3's recurrent synapses, or without CA3, R3 and R3s have no signal (0 for every item; every item new), as for R2. Without DG, CA3 stores the perforant path's own patterns (E8b, item 22) and R3 reads the same synapses as in the intact model. The thinned perforant paths change the starting state and nothing else.
5. **Logged only.** For every item: the largest share of the starting state's cells shared with any one stored pattern, and the share shared with the studied drawing's own pattern (list lookups, never used to decide), beside E8b's logged quantities.
6. **No new parameter.** R3 has no free setting. Its two thresholds are set as R1's and R2's are.

## The test set: drawings E8b's main lists never used

7. **Why a new test set.** The starting-state idea came from E8b's evaluation results at 128 drawings, so those results cannot test it. Every E8b evaluation run logged the starting state's self-support per item; anyone could compute R3s's full outcome on them today. E8c's scored numbers must come from items whose outcome no one has looked at.
8. **The held-out pool.** E8b's 128-drawing evaluation lists (10 seeds, 8 lists each) studied or tested 10,476 of the 13,180 evaluation drawings (659 characters, 20 drawers each). The other 2,704 drawings form the held-out pool; 619 characters have at least two of them and 650 at least one (`python -m trisynapsnet.eval.starting pool`, rebuilt from E8b's list generators). Each E8c evaluation list draws 128 studied characters from those with two held-out drawings (one studied, one spare for a lure), 64 foil characters from the rest with at least one, and every drawing from the pool. Half the studied drawings return as repeats and half as lure sources, as in E8b. Closer lures distort the studied drawing at E8b's five levels with new random draws.
9. **Seeds.** Evaluation seeds 100 to 109, so lists, model wiring (seed 1,000 s + i) and distortions are all new. E8b used seeds 0 to 9.
10. **What this buys.** The scored lists, drawings, wiring and distortions are ones no E8b table summarised. A result that came from the sampling noise in which the idea was spotted, or from the particular drawings E8b's main lists used, will not repeat here.
11. **What it does not buy.** The held-out drawings come from the same 20 alphabets and the same 20 drawers, and every one of them appeared in some other E8b evaluation condition (loads 20 to 256, two and four drawings per character), where the starting state's support was logged but never summarised. They are a different sample of the same population, not a new population. E8c cannot show that R3 behaves the same way on other scripts, other drawers or other kinds of images. Omniglot is the only set with 20 drawers per character, which the other-drawer lures need, and the frozen vision network was trained on Omniglot's background alphabets, so a different image set would change the vision code as well as the test.
12. **Background lists** for calibration are built exactly as E8b's (seeds 0 to 9, full background pool), so the same wiring and lists come back: R1 and R2 there must equal E8b's background signals exactly, a check that E8c's code leaves the model unchanged.
13. **Nobody computes R3 or R3s outcomes from E8b's evaluation files** before E8c's evaluation run is scored. Afterwards, the same scores on E8b's evaluation data may be reported, labelled as the data that suggested the idea.

## Runs

14. **One condition**: 128 drawings, one drawing per character, every arm: intact, no CA3 recurrent connections, no CA3, no DG, perforant path at 75%, 50% and 25% of each CA3 cell's connections, the vision-code baseline, and no hippocampus (every item new, no run). Both lure sets: other-drawer lures in E8's five similarity bins L1 to L5, and closer lures at E8b's five levels C1 to C5. 10 seeds, 8 lists per seed (about 1,000 studied drawings per seed).
15. **Background run** (calibration): `scripts/run_e8c.sh A`, then thresholds (`scripts/make_e8c_table.py --calibrate`, `results/e8c_calibration.json`).
16. **Evaluation run** (the test), scored once: `scripts/run_e8c.sh C`.
17. No load or interference conditions; E8b's curves stand.

## Decision rule and calibration

18. **Two thresholds per readout**, θ_old ≥ θ_new, with E8b's tie rule: above θ_old old, below θ_new new, between similar.
19. **Young adults' criteria** (the main score): on background lists at 128 drawings, θ_old so the intact model's p(old | repeat) is 0.812, θ_new so its p(new | foil) is 0.820 (Stark and colleagues 2013, Table 2, N = 26). Every arm is scored with the intact model's thresholds; the vision-code baseline keeps its own. If θ_new comes out above θ_old, there is no room for similar, and this is reported.
20. **Each arm with its own young-adult thresholds**, from its background run, as a secondary score (not for arms with no CA3 signal).
21. **Older adults' criteria**, a secondary score for the ageing test (prediction 6): for the intact model and the three thinned perforant paths, each with its own thresholds set on background lists to the p(old | repeat) 0.822 and p(new | foil) 0.734 of adults 60 to 74 (same table, N = 29). p(old | lure) is never fitted.
22. Nothing is fitted to p(old | lure), LDI, the similarity curve or any lesion effect.

## Ageing and the perforant path

23. **Basis.** Wilson and colleagues (2006) built their account of hippocampal ageing on loss of entorhinal input with CA3's recurrent synapses spared: in aged rats "the DG receives approximately one-third fewer synaptic contacts from the EC than in young rats", while "synapses onto CA3 cells from their own recurrent collaterals are not reduced in number during aging", so that small changes with age "strengthen the auto-associative network of the CA3 subregion at the cost of processing new information coming in from the entorhinal cortex". In older people, high-resolution diffusion imaging shows a weaker perforant-path signal with no change in a control pathway (Yassa, Muftuler and Stark 2010), and perforant-path integrity correlates with lure discrimination in older adults (r = 0.73, n = 11; Yassa, Mattfeld and colleagues 2011). Older adults are "less likely to call lure items similar and more likely to call them old" (Yassa, Mattfeld and colleagues 2011).
24. **Cautions.** In rats, synaptophysin staining in the perforant-path layer of CA3 fell by about 28% only in aged rats with learning impairment; chronological age alone showed no reliable difference, and bouton density did not change (Smith and colleagues 2000). Ageing also lowers acetylcholine and changes inhibition (Wilson and colleagues 2006; Stark and colleagues 2019). Thinning the perforant path is therefore a stand-in for one part of ageing, entorhinal input loss with recurrent synapses spared, not for ageing as a whole, and the levels 75%, 50% and 25% are E8's, not a measured loss.
25. **Damage.** The one hippocampal-lesion study read found that patients did not call more lures old than controls (Kirwan and colleagues 2012, p(old | lure) 0.38 against 0.37); they called more lures new. Higher p(old | lure) is reported for mild cognitive impairment (64.9%; Stark and colleagues 2013), which is not a lesion. E8c makes no prediction that lesions raise p(old | lure) in people.

## Measures

26. Per readout, lure set and arm, means over the 10 seeds with standard deviations: response rates for repeats, lures and foils; LDI, REC and p(old | lure); p(old | lure), p(similar | lure) and LDI by bin or level; median signal per item type. Under older adults' criteria, the same for the intact and thinned arms.
27. CA3 state, logged only: E8b's (cue size, exact landing of repeats and lures on the studied pattern, foils on any stored pattern, nearest-pattern overlap, the starting state's self-support), plus the starting state's largest overlap with any stored pattern and its overlap with the studied pattern.
28. The R1 and R2 check on background lists (item 12): the largest absolute difference from E8b's background signals.

## Predictions

Written before any E8c run. Scored on means over seeds 100 to 109 on the held-out evaluation lists at 128 drawings, with young adults' criteria and fixed thresholds, closer lures, R3, intact model, unless a prediction says otherwise. Each gives its reason. The labels match the rows `scripts/make_e8c_table.py` prints.

- **1. R3's thresholds leave room for "similar"** at both young-adult rates. Reason: E8b's logged starting-state support separated repeats (median 0.233) from lures and foils (0.049 to 0.052), unlike R2, whose thresholds crossed. Held if θ_new ≤ θ_old on background lists.
- **2a. p(old | lure) rises with lure similarity**, the human direction. Reason: a state's energy grows with its squared overlap with stored patterns (Cortes and colleagues 2010, eq. 3.2), and closer lures start nearer the studied pattern (in E8b, 33.9% of C1 lures and 7.5% of C5 lures settled onto it). Held if p(old | lure) at C1 is above C5 and no step to the next less similar level raises it by more than 0.05. The same test on the other-drawer bins L1 to L5 is reported beside it.
- **2b. LDI does not rise toward less similar lures**, unlike people's. Reason: after DG's pattern separation, a distant lure's starting state shares little with the studied pattern, so R3 treats it like a foil and the answer is new rather than similar, much as human CA3/DG activity treats lures (Bakker and colleagues 2008). Held if LDI fails the rising test (C5 above C1 with no step toward less similar lures lowering it by more than 0.05). Other-drawer bins reported beside it.
- **3. p(old | lure) falls more than 0.15 below young adults' 0.403.** Reason as in 2b: most lures start like foils. Held if p(old | lure) < 0.253.
- **3b. p(new | lure) is above 0.30**, against young adults' 0.083. Held if above 0.30.
- **4. REC is within 0.10 of young adults' 0.786**, on the repeats and foils both lure sets share. Reason: the criteria fix p(old | repeat) near 0.812 on background lists, foils' starting states rarely reach θ_old, and E8b's R1 lost about 0.09 of REC from background to evaluation drawings. Held if REC is between 0.686 and 0.886.
- **5a. Without CA3's recurrent connections, every item is new** (p(old | lure) 0, REC 0). This holds by construction, since R3 reads recurrent synapses, and is stated with the result.
- **5b. Without DG, p(old | lure) is above the intact model's and LDI below it.** Reason: without pattern separation, similar drawings get overlapping stored patterns, so lures' starting states contain more potentiated pairs. This is the direction of the account that ties lure discrimination to DG and CA3 (Yassa and Stark 2011) and of the patient with a lesion mostly of the dentate gyrus, whose LDI was close to zero while repeats and foils were answered as by controls (Baker and colleagues 2016, in `docs/e8_sources.md`).
- **5c. Thinning the perforant path lowers p(old | lure) and REC at each step** (75% at or below intact, 50% at or below 75%, 25% at or below 50%), fixed thresholds. Reason: a weaker perforant cue starts repeats and lures on fewer of the studied pattern's cells, so R3 falls for both. Held at a step if both are at or below the step before.
- **6. Under older adults' criteria, each thinned perforant path calls more lures old than the intact model does under the same criteria** (item 21). Reason: thinning lowers repeats' starting-state support more than lures', which start near foils already, so once the criteria are reset to older adults' hit and correct-rejection rates, lures fall nearer the old threshold: the direction Wilson and colleagues' account predicts for people, reached here through a weaker cue rather than stronger completion. Reported beside it: the distance from adults' 0.546, and p(old | lure) by level, since in people the age gap is smallest for the most similar lures (Yassa, Lacy and colleagues 2011). Held at a level if p(old | lure) is above the intact model's.
- **7. R1 and R2 on the held-out pool repeat E8b's evaluation values within 0.05**: REC 0.724 (R1), LDI 0.006 and p(old | lure) 0.024 (R1, closer lures), p(old | lure) 0.396 (R2, closer lures), REC 0.432 (R2). Reason: the pool is another sample of the same drawings. Held if all five are within 0.05.

R3s is scored with the same rules and reported beside R3; no separate prediction is written for it.

## Not in E8c

- A perforant-path or perirhinal stand-in that reads the energy of the EC code itself, where the familiarity literature places the computation. It would need a recurrent or plastic cortical layer the model does not have.
- Summed CA1 activity as a readout, the signal Hasselmo and Schnell's model (1994) feeds back to the medial septum and one that separates old from new items in Norman's model (2010). It could be tested as a further readout; adding it here would add a fifth readout to the same clean test.
- Load and interference curves under R3.
