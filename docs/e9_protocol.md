# E9 protocol

In the Mnemonic Similarity Task, young adults call 40.3% of similar new pictures old (Stark and colleagues 2013, Table 2). E8b and E8c gave the fully local hippocampus the same test built from Omniglot drawings, and no readout made people's error. Read from CA3's starting state, before attractor dynamics, starting-state familiarity (E8c's R3) recognised studied drawings nearly as well as young adults (REC 0.760 against 0.786) but called no lure old. Read from CA3's final state, final-state support (E8b's R2) called 38.4% of closer lures old in E8c, but only because attractor dynamics carried every cue, foils included, onto some stored pattern (in E8b, repeats ended on their own pattern 99.7% of the time and every lure and foil on some stored pattern), and R2's two thresholds crossed, so it never answered "similar".

In this model DG takes part only in storage. At study it imposes each drawing's pattern on CA3 through the mossy fibres; at test only the perforant path drives CA3, and only for the first round (Treves and Rolls 1992, the scheme the model has followed since E3). Recordings contradict a silent DG at test: in rats, DG and CA3 recorded together during a test both responded, DG separating and CA3 completing (Neunuebel and Knierim 2014), and in human imaging DG holds distinct representations of similar scenes (Berron and colleagues 2016).

E9 asks what CA3 does when DG drives it at recall as well as at storage. It changes one thing in the circuit, the input CA3 receives during attractor dynamics, and adds no readout. It follows Experiment 1 of the research proposal "Dentate Input at Recall and Feedback Inhibition in CA3 as Sources of False Recognition" (`reports/2026-10-21-recognition-errors-proposal/RECOGNITION-ERRORS-PROPOSAL.tex`, hypotheses H1 to H3); where the proposal is silent it follows `docs/e8c_protocol.md` and `docs/e8b_protocol.md`, and where it departs from the proposal it says so under "Departures from the proposal". This file states the biological case, every change and setting with its source, the test set and its limits, the runs and the predictions. It was committed before any run. The sources, with quotes and locations, are in `docs/e9_sources.md`; the human rates are in `docs/e8_sources.md`.

## Terms

- **Mnemonic Similarity Task (MST)**. A person studies pictures once, then sees **repeats** (the same picture), **lures** (a similar picture never shown) and **foils** (new pictures), and answers old, similar or new for each (Stark and colleagues 2013). **LDI** = p(similar | lure) − p(similar | foil). **REC** = p(old | repeat) − p(old | foil). Young adults (20 to 39 years): LDI 0.360, REC 0.786, p(old | lure) 0.403, p(old | foil) 0.026. Adults 60 to 74: p(old | lure) 0.546 (same table).
- **Closer lures** (E8b): the studied drawing itself, rotated, scaled, sheared and displaced at five magnitudes, C1 (most similar) to C5. **Other-drawer lures** (E8): another person's drawing of a studied character, in five similarity bins L1 to L5.
- **Pattern separation** stores similar experiences as distinct patterns; in the model DG does this, giving each drawing its own sparse CA3 pattern. **Attractor dynamics** in CA3: over repeated rounds, each cell sums its input, the 2% most driven cells fire in the next round (k-winners-take-all, the model's stand-in for feedback inhibition), and the rest stay silent, until activity holds still at an **attractor**, a stored pattern or a mixture of them. Rebuilding a whole stored pattern from part of it this way is **pattern completion**.
- **Starting state**: the CA3 cells the perforant path's drive selects for a test drawing, before any recurrent input acts. **Final state**: the state attractor dynamics end in.
- **Units of input.** Every input to a CA3 cell is counted in units of one potentiated recurrent synapse from an active CA3 cell. A cell inside a stored 68-cell pattern receives at most 67 units from the rest of the pattern.
- **The model** is E8c's, unchanged in every part not named below: DG, CA3 and CA1 at human proportions scaled by 1/500 (30,000, 3,400 and 32,000 cells), 2% active, 280 mossy inputs per CA3 cell with fixed random weights, CA3 with clipped binary recurrent synapses, a one-shot perforant path with clipped binary synapses (710 of the 20,449 EC units per CA3 cell), and CA1 learning by behavioural timescale synaptic plasticity (`docs/e8b_protocol.md`). Nothing learns at test, so test items do not affect one another.
- **Readouts**, all E8c's, none new: **R1**, the CA1 comparator, the cosine between the test drawing's EC code and the EC code CA1 returns from CA3's final state; **R2**, final-state support, the share of the final state's potentiated recurrent input that comes from inside it; **R3**, starting-state familiarity, the share of the starting state's cell pairs joined by a potentiated synapse; **R3s**, E8b's self-support formula applied to the starting state. R3 is a stand-in for a signal the literature places in perirhinal cortex (`docs/e8c_protocol.md`).

## The biological case

The sources are read in full in `docs/e9_sources.md`, with pages or sections. In short:

- **DG is active at test.** With DG and CA3 recorded together in behaving rats while local and global cues were put in conflict, "CA3 showed a coherent population response to the conflict (pattern completion), even though its DG inputs were severely disrupted (pattern separation)" (Neunuebel and Knierim 2014, Summary). In human 7-tesla imaging, DG "specifically sustains representations of similar scenes that are less overlapping than in other hippocampal (e.g., CA3) and medial temporal lobe regions" (Berron and colleagues 2016, Abstract).
- **DG output at recall matters for behaviour.** Silencing at test the DG cells active during learning impaired fear-memory expression in mice (Denny and colleagues 2014, Figure 5F); silencing DG during recall "did not impair fear expression in the training context, unless mice had to distinguish between similar feared and neutral contexts" (Bernier and colleagues 2017, Abstract); mice in which the output of old granule cells was inhibited "exhibited deficits in rapid pattern completion" (Nakashiba and colleagues 2012, Summary).
- **Against.** Granule cells in dorsal DG "control exploratory drive and encoding, not retrieval, of contextual fear memories" (Kheirbek and colleagues 2013, Abstract). The two classic models of CA3's two inputs argue that "during retrieval, or at least while initiating retrieval, the mossy fiber input should be absent or strongly reduced with respect to its amplitude during learning" (Treves and Rolls 1992, p. 196), and that if mossy input is too large a share of CA3's input at recall, "pattern completion will suffer" (O'Reilly and McClelland 1994, p. 678). E9 tests DG input at recall at measured strength and reports whether completion of repeats suffers.
- **Timing.** After stimulation of entorhinal axons in vivo, "the initial monosynaptic excitation of pyramidal cells then is followed by weaker excitatory volleys transmitted through the trisynaptic pathway", and "responses of CA3 pyramidal cells often precede those of dentate granule cells" (Yeckel and Berger 1990, Abstract). So the direct perforant input sets CA3's starting state and DG's input arrives after it.
- **EC input lasts while the item is in view.** In macaque entorhinal cortex, visual responses to 500 ms pictures had a mean onset of 181 ms and a mean duration of 293 ms (range 100 to 420 ms; Suzuki, Miller and Desimone 1997, Results); in human entorhinal cortex, neurons responded to 1 s pictures with a mean latency of 392 ms (Mormann and colleagues 2008, Results). EC input therefore lasts hundreds of milliseconds, far longer than the few milliseconds between the monosynaptic and trisynaptic volleys. The model's current recall drops EC input after the first round, which these recordings do not support.
- **Strengths.** In rat slices a CA3 recurrent synapse gives a somatic unitary excitatory postsynaptic potential (EPSP) of 0.56 ± 0.01 mV and a peak conductance of 0.54 ± 0.12 nS (Guzman and colleagues 2016); a mossy-fibre synapse gives 9.6 ± 2.4 mV (Vyleta and colleagues 2016) and about 1 nS, excluding failures (Jonas, Major and Sakmann 1993, Abstract); a model fitted to measured unitary currents predicts 0.34 ± 0.03 mV for a perforant-path synapse at the soma, against 0.56 ± 0.05 mV for a recurrent one in the same model (Baker and colleagues 2011, Table 4). A single mossy EPSP fired a CA3 cell with probability 0.12 ± 0.08 (Vyleta and colleagues 2016), single granule-cell spikes "generally failed to discharge" CA3 cells in vivo while trains succeeded (Henze, Wittner and Buzsáki 2002, Abstract), and post-tetanic potentiation switched mossy synapses "into full detonators for tens of seconds" (Vyleta and colleagues 2016, Abstract). The model has no spike timing, so the effective mossy strength at recall is uncertain; E9 runs two values that bound it (items 6 and 7).

**Verdict.** DG input to CA3 at recall passes the plan's three checks (the proposal's Table 1, scored 18 of 27): the mossy fibres exist in humans and carry input at test, a silent DG contradicts recordings, and no human dissociation rules it out. Two classic models argue against strong mossy input at retrieval. E9 therefore tests it at measured strengths and reports what happens to completion of repeats as well as to lures.

## The changes

1. **DG responds to the test drawing.** At test, DG computes its response to the test drawing exactly as at study: the same fixed random EC-to-DG weights, the 2% most driven granule cells (600 of 30,000) firing, and AHA's record of recently active cells (each cell that fired is inhibited, and the inhibition decays by a factor 0.95 with each later drawing) **frozen at the end of study**, as the proposal sets it. The frozen record is the same for every test item, so test items still do not affect one another. Its consequence: the last drawings studied have their own granule cells partly inhibited at test (the last 13 of 128 at half drive or less), so a repeat's DG response can differ from its study pattern. This record is AHA's engineering stand-in for keeping successive drawings apart; no source read measures it, and the share of each repeat's DG response shared with its study pattern is logged (item 13).
2. **Mossy input at recall.** DG's response reaches CA3 through the same fixed mossy weights used at study (280 inputs per CA3 cell, each weight drawn uniform in [0, 1) and never changed), rescaled so that the mean weight equals the mossy strength in units (items 6 and 7), keeping the fixed random spread.
3. **Timing.** The perforant path alone sets the starting state, exactly as in E8c (k-winners-take-all with E%-max 0.3 on the normalised perforant drive, so a starting state can have fewer than 68 cells). DG's input joins from the first round of attractor dynamics and stays on until the state stops changing (Yeckel and Berger 1990). Starting-state familiarity (R3, R3s) is therefore unchanged by design; item 19 checks it.
4. **EC input stays on.** In every round of attractor dynamics, each CA3 cell also receives its perforant input: 0.61 units for each active EC unit (EC code above zero, the model's presynaptic rule) arriving on a potentiated perforant synapse. Unpotentiated perforant synapses carry nothing, as unpotentiated recurrent synapses carry nothing, since both are clipped binary synapses at zero. In E8c's model this input was dropped after the starting state.
5. **Each round.** Cell j's input in round t + 1 is recurrent (sum over active cells i of W[i, j]) + EC (item 4) + mossy (item 2), and the 68 most driven cells fire (2% of 3,400, E8c's fixed count). Rounds update every cell at once, stop when every item's state stops changing or after 70 rounds (E8c's limit), and each item logs the rounds that changed its state and whether it reached a fixed point.
6. **Main mossy strength, 17.1 units**: 9.6 mV / 0.56 mV, the somatic unitary EPSP of a mossy synapse (Vyleta and colleagues 2016) over a recurrent one (Guzman and colleagues 2016), both rat. Somatic EPSPs include the attenuation from synapse to soma, where firing is decided. The run uses the exact ratio 9.6 / 0.56 = 17.142857.
7. **Lower bound, 1.9 units**: 1 nS / 0.54 nS, peak conductances (Jonas, Major and Sakmann 1993; Guzman and colleagues 2016); exact ratio 1.851852. Run as a second condition.
8. **Perforant strength, 0.61 units**: 0.34 mV / 0.56 mV (Baker and colleagues 2011, Table 4, over Guzman and colleagues 2016; Baker's own model gives 0.56 mV for a recurrent synapse too, so the ratio is the same within that model). Exact ratio 0.607143.
9. **What these strengths imply, before any run.** With 2% of DG active, a CA3 cell's 280 DG inputs carry about 5.6 active ones, about 96 units at 17.1, with a standard deviation near 47 units from the weights' spread; the 68 cells DG's response drives most lie roughly 100 units above the mean. A cell inside a stored pattern gets at most 67 units from the rest of the pattern. EC codes have about 1,270 of 20,449 units active on background drawings (6.2%), so a CA3 cell's 710 perforant inputs carry about 44 active ones, at most about 27 units when every one is potentiated (a repeat, on its own pattern's cells). At 17.1, DG's input should dominate, the basis of H1; at 1.9 every mossy number is about nine times smaller, and recurrent input should dominate. These are expectations from the model's structure, not fitted values.
10. **Active cells stay at 68**, so DG input (and EC input, in the control) is the only difference between conditions.
11. **No new readout.** R1, R2, R3 and R3s are computed as in E8c, R1 and R2 from the final state reached under input. No readout compares a state with a list of stored patterns.
12. **Lesions**, as E8c: without DG (CA3 stores the perforant path's own patterns, E8b item 22, and nothing arrives through the mossy fibres at recall, so this arm receives EC input only), without CA3's recurrent synapses (rounds run on EC and mossy input alone; R2, R3 and R3s have no signal), without CA3 (R1 from CA1's reinstated EC code, unchanged), the perforant path at 75%, 50% and 25% of each CA3 cell's connections (DG and its input untouched), the vision-code baseline, and no hippocampus (every item new). The thinned paths thin the starting state and the EC input during attractor dynamics.
13. **Logged only, never used to decide.** For every item, condition and arm: E8c's CA3 log (starting-state size, whether the final state is exactly the paired studied pattern or any stored pattern, the final state's largest overlap with any stored pattern, the starting state's self-support), plus rounds, fixed point, and the share of the final state's cells in the pattern DG's test response alone would pick (the 68 cells with the most mossy input). Per item: the share of the test drawing's DG response shared with its paired studied drawing's DG pattern (−1 for foils). The "exactly a stored pattern" share for foils, lures and repeats is H1's measure; it reads the stored list, so it is a check, not a readout.

## The test set

14. **What suggested E9.** The question came from the literature (DG active at test, the proposal's mechanism search) and from E8b's and E8c's outcomes on the evaluation alphabets: every cue ended on a stored pattern, and no readout called a lure old. No E9 signal has been computed on any drawing: DG input at recall and EC input during attractor dynamics have never been run.
15. **No untouched drawings remain.** E8b's 128-drawing evaluation lists used 10,476 of the 13,180 evaluation drawings, and E8c's evaluation lists used 2,698 of the other 2,704; 6 drawings remain, no two of one character (computed from both experiments' list generators). A held-out pool like E8c's is no longer possible.
16. **The test set**: every evaluation drawing (20 alphabets, 659 characters, 20 drawers each), lists built by E8b's list generator, with new seeds **200 to 209**, as the proposal sets them. Lists, model wiring (seed 1,000 s + i), DG and mossy weights, and distortions are all new, so every closer lure is a new image. 128 drawings studied per list, one drawing per character, 8 lists per seed; 64 repeats, 64 other-drawer lures and 64 foils per list, plus each lure source's closer lures at C1 to C5.
17. **What this buys.** Every setting in E9 is fixed from measurements or from E8c before any E9 signal exists, and the scored lists, wiring and distortions are new, so a result cannot come from tuning on these items or from the sampling noise of E8b's and E8c's lists. H1 to H3 compare conditions on the same lists, so most of their weight rests on within-list differences.
18. **What it does not buy.** Every evaluation drawing appeared in E8b's or E8c's tests, the alphabets and drawers are the same, and the mechanism was chosen after the model failed on them. The result is a new sample of the same population, not a new population. Omniglot is the only set with 20 drawers per character, which the other-drawer lures need, and the frozen vision network was trained on Omniglot's background alphabets, so another image set would change the vision code as well as the test.

## Reproduction check

19. **Background lists** for calibration are built exactly as E8b's and E8c's (background alphabets, seeds 0 to 9), so the same lists and wiring come back. Before the evaluation run, `scripts/make_e9_table.py --calibrate` checks: (a) under E8c's model, every readout (R1, R2, R3, R3s) on every arm equals E8c's background signals (`results/e8c_background_n128_d1.json`) exactly (largest absolute difference 0); (b) the young-adult thresholds for E8c's model equal E8c's (`results/e8c_calibration.json`); (c) R3 and R3s are identical across all four conditions, on every arm. If any check fails, the calibration file records it and `scripts/run_e9.sh C` stops before the evaluation run.

## Decision rule and calibration

20. **Two thresholds per readout and condition**, θ_old ≥ θ_new, with E8b's tie rule: above θ_old old, below θ_new new, between similar. If θ_new comes out above θ_old the thresholds cross, there is no room for similar, and they are reported and used as set.
21. **Young adults' criteria** (the main score): on background lists at 128 drawings, for each condition, θ_old so the intact model's p(old | repeat) is 0.812 and θ_new so its p(new | foil) is 0.820 (Stark and colleagues 2013, Table 2). Every arm of a condition is scored with that condition's intact thresholds, as a patient keeps a healthy person's criteria; the vision-code baseline keeps its own.
22. **Each arm with its own young-adult thresholds**, from its background run, as a secondary score (not for arms with no CA3 signal).
23. **Older adults' criteria**, a secondary score: for the intact model and the three thinned perforant paths in each condition, each with its own thresholds set to adults 60 to 74's p(old | repeat) 0.822 and p(new | foil) 0.734 (same table).
24. Nothing is fitted to p(old | lure), LDI, the similarity curve, any lesion effect or the share of items ending on stored patterns. The two mossy strengths and the perforant strength are fixed by items 6 to 8.

## Runs

25. **Background run** (calibration): `scripts/run_e9.sh A`: 128 drawings, every arm, all four conditions, both lure sets, seeds 0 to 9, 8 lists per seed; then `scripts/make_e9_table.py --calibrate` writes `results/e9_calibration.json` and the item-19 checks.
26. **Evaluation run** (the test), scored once: `scripts/run_e9.sh C`, which first refuses to run unless the item-19 checks passed; evaluation alphabets, seeds 200 to 209; then `scripts/make_e9_table.py` writes `results/e9_scores.json` and `results/e9.md`.
27. One result file per condition and split (`results/e9_<split>_n128_d1_<condition>.json`); E8c's runner and result files are not changed.
28. At most two runs at once, each under 4 GB resident, with a watchdog that stops every run if free memory falls below 20% or a run passes 4 GB, as in E8b and E8c.

## Measures

29. Per readout, condition, lure set and arm, means over the 10 seeds with standard deviations: response rates for repeats, lures and foils; LDI, REC and p(old | lure); p(old | lure), p(similar | lure) and LDI by level or bin; median signal per item type. Under older adults' criteria, the same for the intact and thinned arms.
30. CA3, logged only (item 13), per condition and arm: the share of repeats ending exactly on their own pattern; the share of repeats, closer lures, other-drawer lures and foils ending exactly on any stored pattern; the share of closer lures ending on their studied pattern, by level; rounds that changed the state; items with no fixed point; the final state's share in DG's own pattern; and the DG overlap between each repeat or lure and its studied drawing.
31. Conditions share seeds, lists and wiring, so each condition contrast in H1 and in the lesion rows is also reported per seed, with the number of seeds showing the predicted sign and a two-sided sign test. The margin rule in each prediction decides.

## Predictions

Written before any E9 run. Scored on means over seeds 200 to 209 on the evaluation lists at 128 drawings, closer lures, young adults' criteria with each condition's fixed thresholds, intact model, unless a prediction says otherwise. H1 to H3 are the proposal's, quoted; each is scored as the proposal's analysis plan sets it: **supported** if the alternative's conditions all hold, **falsified** if the null's hold, **weakened** otherwise. "Falls from C1 to C5" means C1 is above C5 and no step to a less similar level rises by more than 0.05. H1 to H3 are scored at 17.1 units against the EC-on control; the 1.9-unit condition is scored by the same rules and reported beside them. The labels match the rows `scripts/make_e9_table.py` prints.

- **H1. DG input at recall changes where attractor dynamics end.** "Null: the share of foils whose final CA3 state is a stored pattern stays within 0.10 of the control's (entorhinal input kept on, no DG input). Alternative: it falls by more than 0.10. Expected: below one half, with repeats still ending on their own pattern at least 95% of the time, because DG keeps separating at test (Neunuebel and Knierim 2014) and at measured strength its input exceeds what a stored pattern's recurrent synapses supply." Scored on the foils of the evaluation lists (shared by both lure sets). The expected values (below one half; repeats at least 95%) are reported beside the verdict; item 1's frozen record may lower the repeats' share for the last drawings studied, and that share is reported by study position.
- **H2. DG input at recall does not by itself produce people's false recognition.** "Null: under every readout, old answers to lures stay below 0.253, more than 0.15 below young adults' 0.403. Alternative: under some readout they lie within 0.15 of 0.403 and fall from C1 to C5, with old answers to foils at most 0.10. Expected: the null, since CA3 and DG treat lures much like new items (Bakker and colleagues 2008) and a recall-based hippocampal signal stays at floor for lures until they are very similar (Norman and O'Reilly 2003)." Every readout (R1, R2, R3, R3s) is primary for H2. Other-drawer lures are scored by the same rule and reported.
- **H3. Final-state support regains room for a similar answer.** "Null: its thresholds still cross. Alternative: they no longer cross. Expected: the alternative, as foils' final states stop being stored patterns." Scored on R2's young-adult thresholds for the intact model at 17.1 units on background lists; the EC-on control's and E8c's model's thresholds are reported beside them.

Further predictions, in E8c's form (not in the proposal):

- **4. Starting-state familiarity is the same in every condition** (item 3). Held if R3 and R3s differ by 0 between conditions on every item of the evaluation lists. Reason: the starting state is set before DG's or EC's later input acts.
- **5. E8c's model on E9's lists repeats E8c's evaluation values within 0.05**: R3 REC 0.760, LDI 0.415 and p(old | lure) 0 (closer lures); R1 REC 0.728; R2 p(old | lure) 0.384 (closer lures). Reason: the full evaluation pool and E8c's held-out pool are samples of the same drawings, and E8c's own replication of E8b held within 0.02. Held if all five are within 0.05.
- **6. Without DG, p(old | lure) is above the intact model's under R2 and under R3**, at 17.1 units. Reason: without pattern separation, similar drawings get overlapping stored patterns, so lures start and end on states rich in potentiated pairs, as in E8c (R3 0.999 without DG), and without DG at recall nothing pulls a lure's final state toward its own pattern. This is also the direction of the patient with a lesion mostly of DG, whose LDI was near zero (Baker and colleagues 2016, `docs/e8_sources.md`). Held if both hold.
- **7. Without CA3's recurrent synapses, the CA3 readouts (R2, R3, R3s) call every item new**, in every condition. This holds by construction and is stated with the result.
- **8. With DG at recall at 17.1 units, thinning the perforant path lowers R3's REC at each step but leaves R1's and R2's REC within 0.10 of the intact model's at every level.** Reason: thinning weakens the starting state, which R3 reads (E8c: REC 0.595, 0.289, 0.007), but at 17.1 units DG's input, which thinning does not touch, decides the final state that R1 and R2 read. Held if R3's REC is at or below the step before at 75%, 50% and 25%, and R1's and R2's REC are within 0.10 of the intact model's at all three.
- **9. Under older adults' criteria, no thinned perforant path at 17.1 units makes the older-adult error under any readout**: p(old | lure) within 0.15 of adults 60 to 74's 0.546 with p(old | foil) at most 0.10. Reason: E9 adds no mechanism by which a weaker perforant path makes lures' final states more like their studied patterns, since the DG input that sets those states is not thinned; the proposal places the ageing test in its second experiment, with inhibition. Held if no thinned path, under any readout, meets both conditions.

## Departures from the proposal

- **A fourth condition, E8c's model**, recalled on the same studies, so the reproduction check (item 19) covers every readout and arm, not only starting-state familiarity as the proposal states. It changes nothing the proposal scores.
- **Exact ratios.** The strengths are run as 9.6 / 0.56, 1 / 0.54 and 0.34 / 0.56 rather than the rounded 17.1, 1.9 and 0.61 the proposal prints; the labels keep the rounded values.
- **Arms.** The proposal names the no-DG, no-recurrent and thinned-path arms; E9 keeps E8c's full set (also no CA3, the vision-code baseline and no hippocampus) so every E8c table has its E9 counterpart.
- **Older adults' criteria and predictions 4 to 9** are added in E8c's form. The proposal places the ageing test in its second experiment; prediction 9 states what E9 expects without it.
- **Perforant units.** The proposal values a perforant synapse at 0.61 units; E9 counts only potentiated perforant synapses, since unpotentiated clipped binary synapses are zero in the model, as unpotentiated recurrent synapses are.

## Not in E9
