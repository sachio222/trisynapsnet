# E3 results

E3 replaces PR, the path from entorhinal cortex (EC) into CA3 that AHA trains by backpropagation, with a perforant path that learns each drawing in one presentation, and asks whether CA3's settling then helps end to end. The protocol, with every choice, source and free parameter, is `docs/e3_protocol.md`; the numbers come from `results/e3_*.json`. Built by `scripts/make_e3_table.py`.

**Main result.** With the perforant path, CA3's settling turns a partial cue into the exact stored pattern, and BTSP CA1 then returns the right drawing's EC code far more often: on the instance task, matched test-to-study, settling adds up to +18.2 points at the CA1 stage (covariance perforant path, clean drawings, higher in 10 of 10 seeds). Scored at CA3 itself it adds at most +1.4 points, because matching a CA3 state against the stored patterns already finds the nearest memory. The fully local model recognises a new drawing of a studied character far less often than the PR model: 29.8% against 65.8% on Lake's runs at the CA1 stage, matched test-to-study.

## Terms

- **DG**, the dentate gyrus, gives each drawing a sparse pattern; CA3 stores it. Here 5 of 225 cells are on (2%), with 10 (4.4%, AHA's value) as a check.
- **Settling**: CA3 updates its cells from their recurrent input, keeping the k most driven, until the state stops changing. Settling from a cue with missing or wrong cells onto the full stored pattern is **pattern completion**.
- **Perforant path**: EC to CA3. Each CA3 cell hears from 710 of the 20,449 EC units (3,750 synapses per rat CA3 cell over 108,000 rat EC layer II cells), and the synapses learn the EC code against the CA3 pattern DG imposes, with clipped binary synapses or the covariance rule. Its drive picks the cue cells.
- **No settling** passes the cue straight on to CA1.
- **CA3 stage, CA1 stage, PM stage**: which state is matched. CA1 is E4's one-shot BTSP CA1 (2,118 cells), which returns an EC code; PM is AHA's CA1, trained to rebuild pixels, as a reference.
- **Test-to-study (T→S)**: each test drawing's state picks the nearest studied state, the way a cue retrieves a memory. **Study-to-test (S→T)**: each studied state picks the nearest test state, AHA's direction. Values are means over seeds ± one standard deviation, in percent.
- **Right cells in cue**: the share of the right stored pattern's k cells that the cue has on. **Exact recall**: the final CA3 state equals the right stored pattern.

## What the numbers show

- **The perforant cue finds the exact studied drawing but rarely a new drawing of it.** On the instance task the clipped binary cue holds 99.5% of the right pattern's cells for clean drawings (covariance 41.6%). On Lake's runs, where the test drawing is by another person, the stored pattern nearest the cue is the right one for 30.2% (clipped binary) and 27.5% (covariance) of drawings, against 63.0% and 67.3% for the whole vision code matched directly.
- **Settling completes the perforant cue, and CA1 needs the completed pattern.** At occlusion 0.3 the clipped binary cue holds 83.5% of the right cells and is exact for 54.7% of drawings; after settling, 93.0% and 91.6%. Matched at CA3, the partial cue already points to the right memory (93.0% T→S), but CA1 reading it returns the right code for only 80.0%; from the settled state, 91.7%. With PR's cue, which is already a full pattern near the right memory, settling adds at most +2.6 points at CA1.
- **The gain needs CA3 below capacity.** In the load runs, settling's largest CA1 gain T→S on clean and occluded drawings is +15.3, +17.4, +6.4 and -1.2 points at 20, 50, 100 and 200 drawings. Exact recall of clean drawings after clipped binary settling falls from 99.9% to 99.2%, 92.3% and 47.1%.
- **Matched study-to-test, settling costs up to 22.4 points at CA3** for the perforant arms. Settled states of different test drawings coincide, and a studied drawing searching among them meets ties, as in E1b.
- **Noise defeats the perforant cue sooner than PR.** At noise 0.3 on the instance task the clipped binary cue holds 11.1% of the right cells, PR's top cells 49.2%.
- **More active cells do not rescue new drawings.** At 4.4% the perforant arms reach 30.6% (covariance) and 26.7% (clipped binary) on Lake's runs at CA3, T→S, against 27.5% and 30.3% at 2%; PR with covariance settling reaches 62.7%. Settling's CA1 gain on the instance task holds: +15.4 points (clipped binary perforant path, occlusion 0.5).
- **Sparse wiring explains part of the gap on new drawings.** On the background validation runs, connecting every CA3 cell to all 20,449 EC units (a diagnostic added after the runs) lifts every perforant arm by 10 to 14 points at CA3, T→S: clipped binary with settling from 39.0% to 49.5%, covariance with settling from 35.9% to 49.6%, clipped binary without settling from 39.2% to 49.4%. PR reaches 68.3%.

## Perforant settings chosen on background alphabets

Score: mean CA3-stage accuracy over the clean validation runs and the instance runs clean and at occlusion and noise 0.3, 0.5 and 0.7, both directions, settling and no settling weighted equally, 5 seeds. Chosen: `wil_pos_norm_e0.3` for clipped binary synapses (0.4160) and `cov_norm_kwta` for the covariance rule (0.2454). Setting names give the rule, then for clipped binary whether an EC unit counts as active above zero (pos) or above its background mean (mean), then raw or normalised drive, then plain k-WTA or the E%-max share. For clipped binary synapses, E%-max 0.3 beat plain k-WTA by 0.0002, a tie in practice. The full grid is in `results/e3_selection.md`.
