# E4: one-shot learning in CA1

AHA's CA1 (PM) learns by backpropagation, 60 optimiser steps over the study set, and
rebuilds pixels. E4 replaces it with a CA1 that learns each drawing in one presentation
by behavioural timescale synaptic plasticity (BTSP), using Wu and Maass's binary rule,
and returns the drawing's EC code, the vision network's 20,449 numbers, through
synapses that grow with CA1 and EC activity. A backpropagation network trained like PM
but with the EC code as its target compares in the same space.

Terms used below:

- **Identification:** the stored EC code nearest the returned one, by cosine, belongs to
  the right drawing. Ties count 1 over the number tied. Chance is 1 over the load.
- **Exact pattern:** CA1 receives the stored CA3 pattern itself.
- **Damaged cue:** E1c's damaged copy of the stored pattern, passed to CA1 as it is.
  **After CA3:** the same copy after CA3 settles with the covariance rule or clipped
  binary synapses.
- **CA1 stage:** on Lake's runs and the instance task, matching on CA1's output.

Every choice is listed in `docs/e4_protocol.md`. Made by `scripts/make_e4_table.py`.

## Predictions

Written in the plan before the runs. The plan names one baseline: a backpropagation network trained as PM is trained, with the EC code as its target (`backprop_ec`). It sits at chance at every load, because PM's L2 penalty outweighs the error signal when the target is an EC code, about 19 times smaller than pixels. A comparison with it tells nothing about BTSP, so each prediction is also judged against the same network without its L2 penalty, the informative comparison, and with the EC target scaled up 19.3 times, as a check. Identification from the exact stored pattern, percent.

| Load | BTSP, 2,118 cells | BTSP, 375 cells | Backprop, PM's training (as planned) | Backprop, no L2 penalty | Backprop, target scaled 19.3x |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20 | 99.0 | 97.0 | 5.0 | 100.0 | 41.0 |
| 50 | 98.6 | 92.0 | 2.0 | 99.8 | 2.6 |
| 100 | 94.8 | 72.4 | 1.0 | 81.4 | 1.0 |
| 200 | 65.1 | 35.3 | 0.5 | 34.8 | 0.5 |

**Prediction 1.** At 20 drawings, BTSP CA1 identifies the right drawing as often as the baseline, within 3 points, after one presentation.

- Against the planned baseline: failed at both sizes in the letter, and uninformative. BTSP scores 99.0 (2,118 cells) and 97.0 (375 cells); the planned baseline scores 5.0, chance, so BTSP is far more than 3 points ahead.
- Against backpropagation without the L2 penalty, the informative comparison: held at 2,118 cells (99.0 against 100.0) and held at 375 cells (97.0 against 100.0, a gap of exactly 3.0 points at the table's precision).
- Against the scaled target, as a check: failed; that network scores 41.0, so BTSP is far ahead.

**Prediction 2.** Past 50 drawings BTSP holds up better: higher identification at 100 and 200 drawings.

- Against the planned baseline: held at both sizes, and uninformative, since that network is at chance at every load.
- Against backpropagation without the L2 penalty: held at 2,118 cells (94.8 and 65.1 against 81.4 and 34.8); failed at 375 cells, which is lower at 100 drawings (72.4 against 81.4) and level at 200 (35.3 against 34.8).
- Against the scaled target: held at both sizes; that network is at chance from 50 drawings on.
- PM itself, rebuilding pixels without its L2 penalty, identifies 100% up to 200 drawings (table below). That readout is in pixel space, so it does not enter the verdicts, but backpropagation is not limited to the numbers in this table.

**Prediction 3.** With CA3 completing partial cues, BTSP CA1's output from a partial cue approaches its output from the exact pattern, below CA3's capacity.

- Held at both sizes. At 2,118 cells and 20 drawings, BTSP identifies 99.0 from the exact pattern; from cues missing 40% or 80% of their active cells it identifies 99.5 and 99.0 after covariance-rule settling, against 96.5 and 89.0 from the raw cue. At 50 drawings: 98.6 exact, 98.6 and 92.2 after settling, 94.4 and 55.6 raw. At 375 cells and 20 drawings: 97.0 exact, 97.5 and 96.5 after settling.
- The gap opens as CA3 nears capacity and follows CA3's exact recall. At 100 drawings with 80% removed, CA3 recalls 49.7% of cues exactly and BTSP identifies 55.8 after settling against 94.8 exact. At 200 drawings, past CA3's capacity, settling hurts: 24.7 after settling against 41.1 from the raw cue with 40% removed.
- The completion comes from CA3: backpropagation without the L2 penalty gains the same way, from 31.0 raw to 99.5 after settling at 20 drawings with 80% removed.

**Prediction 4.** CA1-stage accuracy on Lake's runs stays within 3 points of the CA3 stage.

- Held at both sizes and in both matching directions; the largest gap is 2.1 points. CA1 against CA3: 2,118 cells study-to-test: 55.8 against 53.8 (+2.1); 2,118 cells test-to-study: 62.4 against 62.7 (-0.2); 375 cells study-to-test: 54.6 against 53.8 (+0.8); 375 cells test-to-study: 61.7 against 62.7 (-0.9).
- On the instance task, 2,118-cell BTSP stays within 1.4 points of CA3 at every level. 375-cell BTSP loses up to 7.1 points, most on clean and lightly occluded drawings, where the 20 drawings are all of one character and their EC codes are alike.

## Other findings

- **PM's rising error floor comes from its L2 penalty.** E1c found that CA1's error from the exact stored pattern rises from 0.0046 at 20 drawings to 0.0363 at 100, and read it as CA1's capacity. The same network with the same 60 steps but no L2 penalty scores 0.0006 at 20 drawings, 0.0020 at 100 and 0.0145 at 500, and identifies 99.5% of drawings at 500. With the penalty it identifies 13.8% at 100.
- **A larger CA3 helps BTSP far more than backpropagation.** At 1,000 CA3 cells with 44 active, BTSP with 9,412 CA1 cells identifies 99.3% at 200 drawings and 39.7% at 500; backpropagation without the L2 penalty identifies 3.7% and 0.2%. Each BTSP cell then sees 44 active inputs instead of 10.
- **Matching can score a network that returns almost nothing.** The planned baseline is at chance at identifying drawings from their CA3 patterns, yet its CA1 stage scores 53.5% on Lake's runs, close to CA3's 53.8%. Normalised matching compares the network's outputs for study and test drawings with each other, and the small differences between near-constant outputs still follow the CA3 states.
